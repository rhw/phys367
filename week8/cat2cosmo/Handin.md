Take a look at any one of the two exercises below. Open the bias_explorer.html in a browser and select reset everything to remove all systematics.

## Exercise 1: Understanding baryonic feedback in WL constraints

1. First increase the baryonic dip depth to the maximum, and keep the scale at log10(k) = 1. Use the selector on the left to pick-out just xi_plus. Then swap and pick-out just xi_minus. In which case is the constraint most biased, and why? Change the scale to log10(k) = 2. What about now?

2. Let's use the case where log10(k) = 0.3. Is the shift/bias in the inferred S8 positive or negative? Argue why the direction is expected. Use the fact that baryonic feedback on average tends to smooth structure.


## Exercise 2: Coupling different systematics in 3x2pt

1. Turn off all systematics using the reset option at the bottom left. Now pick just w_theta (galaxy clustering) and galaxy-galaxy lensing (gamma_t); so, no WL. This is also called the 2x2pt analysis. Now, vary --- one at a time --- the systematics corresponding to the source galaxy redshift, the source galaxy shear bias, and the intrinsic alignment (IA) amplitude. Is the direction of the bias in S8 similar/different across the three cases? What does that tell you about our ability to distinguish between one effect compared to another?
   
2. Pick all four datavectors, and reset all systematics once again. Then set the baryon amplitude to maximum. Now, slide the A_IA value until you find a value that minimizes the parameter bias in the inferred S8. Can systematics cancel each other out? Now keep your setup but unselect w_theta and gamma_t from the analysis. Is your parameter bias still small?
